Our story

It started on a trip to India.

ABLE Initiatives began with something our founder couldn't stop thinking about after visiting family: the distance between what his cousins were capable of and what they were actually going to get the chance to do.

SD

The founder

Same potential. Different odds.

Siddharth Dodda started ABLE Initiatives after a trip to India to see family.

He spent that trip around his cousins and their friends, and what struck him wasn't that they were behind. It was the opposite. They were as sharp as anyone he knew at home, and several of them sharper. They asked better questions. They worked harder, for less.

What they didn't have was everything that sits around ability and turns it into an outcome. No practice tests. Nobody who had already been through an application and could explain how it actually worked. No sessions to sit in on, no materials to take home afterwards, and no way to buy any of it.

The difference between him and them was never talent. It was access, and which side of that line each of them happened to be born on.

He came home and started ABLE so that difference would decide a little less.

What we do about it

Put the resources in the room, and take the price off.

Everything ABLE runs comes back to that trip. Workshops, webinars and guest speaker sessions across college prep, health and business — the study plans, the practice, the person who has already walked the path and will explain it to you.

Those things exist almost everywhere. They're just usually available for a price. We wanted them available without one: no fee, no application, nothing to qualify for, and the materials are yours to keep afterwards.

What we believe

The principles behind how we run ABLE

$0

Free, no exceptions

No tuition, no application fee, no premium tier. Every program stays free for every student, every time.

ALL

Open to everyone

No application, no eligibility screen, no waitlist. If you want to be in the room, you're in.

SL

Student-led

Run by high schoolers and college students volunteering alongside their own classes, not a top-down institution.
